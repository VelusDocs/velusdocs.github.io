The Sugar Industry Paid Harvard to Blame Fat. It Worked.

Quoted statements in the video and where each can be read. Documents shown on screen are reconstructions of the quoted text. Quotes are given as they appear in the video; a cut is marked with an ellipsis.

Primary source for nearly every quote: Kearns CE, Schmidt LA, Glantz SA. "Sugar Industry and Coronary Heart Disease Research: A Historical Analysis of Internal Industry Documents." JAMA Internal Medicine 2016;176(11):1680-1685. https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2016.5394 (free full text on PubMed Central, search the title). Called "K16" below.

# Statement in the video Source
1 Nov 1966, Hickson to Hegsted: "Let me assure you this is quite what we had in mind and we look forward to its appearance in print." K16
2 1954 speech by the Sugar Research Foundation president to sugar beet technologists, "What's New in Sugar Research": "a tremendous improvement in general health" K16
3 K16's reading of that speech: it "identified a strategic opportunity for the sugar industry" K16
4 1962 AMA report via the foundation's scientific advisory board minutes: the kind of carbohydrate "should be watched carefully" K16
5 Dec 1964 Hickson memo: "from a number of laboratories of greater or lesser repute"; "Then we can publish the data and refute our detractors." K16
6 June 1965 Annals of Internal Medicine editorial: if triglycerides are a risk factor, then "sucrose must be atherogenic" K16
7 Project 226 approved 13 July 1965, two days after the Herald Tribune page of 11 July; Hickson visited Hegsted on 1 July K16
8 15 July 1965 offer: a review of papers finding "some special metabolic peril in sucrose and, in particular, fructose"; half paid at the start, the rest "when you inform me that the article has been accepted for publication" K16
9 30 July 1965: "Our particular interest ..."; "I will be disappointed if this aspect is drowned out in a cascade of review and general interpretation." K16
10 Hegsted, 10 Aug 1965: "We are well aware of your particular interest in carbohydrate and will cover this as well as we can." K16
11 Total paid: $6,500 (about $48,900 in 2016 dollars) K16
12 Apr 1966, Hegsted: "Every time the Iowa group publishes a paper we have to rework a section in rebuttal." K16
13 Sept 1966 draft exchange ("Am I going to get another copy of the draft shortly?"); final draft received 25 Oct; "It is not clear whether the SRF commented on or edited drafts" K16
14 The 1967 review: McGandy RB, Hegsted DM, Stare FJ. "Dietary fats, carbohydrates and atherosclerotic vascular disease." New England Journal of Medicine 1967;277:186-192 and 242-247 NEJM archive
15 The review discounted Yudkin's and the Iowa group's studies; the funding was not disclosed (NEJM has required full disclosure since 1984) K16
16 No fabricated data has been found, and no document shows the sponsor changed the review K16 (limitations)
17 The industry shaped the 1971 National Caries Program toward interventions other than limiting sugar Kearns CE, Glantz SA, Schmidt LA. PLoS Medicine 2015 (National Caries Program analysis)
18 The foundation began its heart-disease research in 1965 "to protect market share" K16 (discussion)
19 Sugar Association, same day: "should have exercised greater transparency in all of its research activities"; sugar "does not have a unique role in heart disease" Sugar Association statement, Sept 2016 (sugar.org)
20 1977 Dietary Goals for the United States (Senate Select Committee on Nutrition): "Reduce the consumption of refined and other processed sugars by about 45 percent" US Senate committee print, 1977
21 Cutting saturated fat lowered combined cardiovascular events by about 17% Hooper L et al. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, 2020
22 Health bodies advise limiting both added sugar and saturated fat WHO guideline on sugars intake, 2015; national dietary guidelines

This video is about the history of research funding, not dietary advice. For nutrition guidance, follow your doctor and official health bodies.
